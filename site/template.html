How to read this

% below usual measures a price against its own recent history — how far it sits below the average it has traded at. It is NOT a discount: a business worth 40% less, priced 40% lower, reads exactly like an unchanged business priced 40% lower.

Quality is this year's verdict from six gates on audited annual filings — profit, returns, cash, debt, margins, revenue. Uncertain is not a bad score: it means too little filing history to judge at all.

vs 10m / 2m avg are the two figures the main number is built from. The 2-month average catches up to a new price level in about two months; the 10-month one takes far longer. Both wide means the fall is happening now; only the 10-month wide means it happened months ago.

Cheaper or pricier compares today's price against what the company's own earnings have historically cost — not against other companies. A price can fall a long way and still be pricier than this company usually is.

Where these come from. The S&P 500 holds {{UNIVERSE}} companies, of which {{REITS}} are REITs — property trusts, excluded because the quality gates measure things that do not apply to them. The remaining {{TESTED}} are tested against six gates on audited SEC filings; {{ELIGIBLE}} clear every one. {{COUNT}} of those {{VERB}} currently more than {{BAR}}% below their usual price, which is what the page shows by default.

How current this is. The quality gates read ANNUAL filings, so a verdict can be two to twelve months old. Prices are from the last close. The quarterly figures behind "cheaper or pricier" carry their own date inside each row.
